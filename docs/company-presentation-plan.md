## Purpose and audience

Give senior management and functional heads a concrete view of what reviewed AI assistance could do across Big Tiny, who benefits, which records it needs and which decisions remain with people. End with a practical choice of one pilot. This is a discussion proposal; the twenty functional areas are not a verified organisation chart.

The authorised delivery is a narrated company-wide presentation with a female Singapore English voice, accompanied by editable slides, a browser-viewable presentation, subtitles and a transcript. Continue the existing 28-slide draft and visual production approach. ## Management prioritisation

1. **First candidate: operations handover and maintenance follow-up.** This is closest to the demonstrated note → proposal → review → approved task-memory workflow. A pilot still needs an operational owner, representative authorised examples and a comparison against existing work. A daily handover report is a proposed addition, not something already demonstrated.
2. **Next candidates: sourced SOP lookup or procurement quote comparison.** Select only if approved, current source material and a named reviewer are available. SOP lookup requires reliable permissions and revisions; quote comparison requires like-for-like scope and checked arithmetic.
3. **Later: cross-system booking, finance, owner reporting and launch coordination.** These depend on stable identifiers, freshness, access controls and reconciliation across source systems. Their attractive cross-team benefits do not establish readiness to integrate.

Do not present a fabricated ROI, fixed deployment schedule or scored department ranking. Use a short qualitative selection check: recurring staff effort, reviewable output, available authorised data, tolerable error consequences and measurable customer relevance. A deterministic checklist or report may be the best tool.

The closing decision should request one workflow owner, authorised sample records, an agreed baseline and success measures, and a pilot review date. Compare manual work, a simpler single-prompt/checklist alternative and the proposed workflow. Measure total active staff time including checking/corrections, missed actions, wrong matches and unsupported statements; include an appropriate guest/owner/partner outcome. Expand, revise or stop based on evidence.

## Accuracy boundaries to preserve

- Current POC: one pasted note, three sequential roles using the same on-prem model, local task/history storage and explicit approval for task changes. Notes and outputs persist before approval.
- It does not currently connect to departmental systems, implement shared company memory, enforce multi-user permissions, send messages or operate unattended. Cloning creates independent local memory.
- The company-wide tools, source integrations and cross-team stories are proposals. The stories use fictional situations and are not claims about Big Tiny incidents.
- Source retrieval requires authorised records, identifiers, freshness and access enforcement. A prompt cannot enforce access control. Shared deployment needs authentication, backups and audit ownership.
- A task marked complete does not establish physical readiness, safety, quality release or compliance. Relevant people retain those decisions.
- Arithmetic and reconciliations should be checked outside the language model. Owner communications must not invent financial-return promises. Environmental claims need supporting measurements and methods.
- Public company context supports discussing the business lifecycle, not asserting internal software, workflows, headcount or proven savings. Link the company sources already identified in the catalogue.

## Remaining discovery gaps

These are future pilot inputs, not reasons to block this presentation: actual department ownership; existing booking/accounting/CRM/maintenance/document systems; data permissions and retention; representative work volume and effort; critical error definitions; source quality; customer communication standards; and owners' or landowners' contractual distinctions. Functional leaders should identify them during selection.
